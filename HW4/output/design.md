# Design

The goal: make Campus Customs feel like a real Yale storefront that is pleasant to browse, easy to trust, and easy to buy from. Everything below is visible in the running site.

## Fonts

- **What changed:** the whole site is set in a Times New Roman style serif (falling back to Times and Georgia), with headings in bold and the second half of the hero headline in italic. Small, letter-spaced capital "eyebrow" labels sit above headings, and prices are in bold serif.
- **Why it helps:** serif type with small caps reads as Yale and as an established shop, not a generic web page. Shoppers trust a brand that looks consistent, and the clear size jump between eyebrow, heading and body text tells them where to look first.

## Color

- **What changed:** Yale blue for the navigation, hero and buttons on a warm cream page, with thin gold rules, a gold double line under the nav bar, and gold highlight strips for promos and low-stock tags.
- **Why it helps:** the cream and gold warm up the blue so it feels like a bookstore rather than an office tool, and the gold is reserved for things we want noticed: the game-day promo, "few sizes left" tags and the assistant's picks.

## Hierarchy

- **What changed:** the Home page now reads top to bottom as one clear story: a big headline and two buttons, a game-day promo strip, "Fan favorites", gear categories, three pillars, then the store address. Each section has an eyebrow label and one heading. The Products page opens with filter chips (Hoodies, Sweatshirts, T-shirts, Jackets and zips, More), with counts.
- **Why it helps:** one primary action per screen ("Shop the collection") and a quick way to narrow 100 products means shoppers find something sooner and keep browsing instead of leaving.

## Emotion

- **What changed:** warm, direct copy ("Wear the blue. Carry the blue."), a framed three-photo collage in the hero, the Yale Y, and the Handsome Dan bulldog trotting along the bottom of every page. The Home page ends with "Say hello in person" and the New Haven address.
- **Why it helps:** pride and personality are what Yale gear is really selling. The mascot and the collage give the site character that a shopper remembers and tells friends about.

## Product presentation

- **What changed:** each product sits in a framed "print" on a cream mat with its kind in small caps, name, a short description, a bold price, and color swatches. A gold "Few sizes left" or red "Sold out" tag appears when stock is low. Cards lift on hover, and every product page has a size picker and a Buy now box with a gold offset shadow.
- **Why it helps:** shoppers see color, price and urgency without clicking, which speeds up decisions and creates honest reasons to act now.

## Shop assistant chat

- **What changed:** the launcher is a Y badge button, "Ask the shop assistant". The window has a blue header with a small Handsome Dan portrait, cream background, a bulldog avatar next to each assistant message, one-tap starter questions ("What hoodies do you have?"), product cards with Buy now buttons, and a "Thinking… / Checking stock…" status while it works.
- **Why it helps:** the starter questions remove the blank-page problem so more visitors actually use the assistant, and the in-chat Buy now button turns an answer into an order in one click.

## Pillars

- **What changed:** "Three pillars" (Honest stock, Made for Bulldogs, Right here in New Haven) are drawn as stone columns with a capital, a fluted shaft, a base and a gold Roman numeral.
- **Why it helps:** it says what makes this store different in three short lines, and the classical columns tie to Yale's architecture so the page feels distinctive rather than a template.
